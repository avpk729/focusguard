FocusGuard Privacy Policy

Last updated: August 22, 2026

FocusGuard (“the Extension”) helps you block distracting websites during scheduled focus hours. This policy explains what data the Extension handles.

Data we collect

FocusGuard does not collect, sell, or share personal information. There is no account system and no analytics backend operated by us.

Data stored on your device

The Extension uses Chrome’s local storage APIs to keep settings on your device only, such as:

  • Whether blocking is enabled
  • Which sites you chose to block
  • Schedule times and days
  • An optional password you set to lock settings changes
  • An optional timed-detox end time, if you start a 1-day, 5-day, or 7-day lock

This data never leaves your browser unless you sync it yourself through your Google account’s Chrome sync (if enabled). We do not receive a copy.

Permissions

  • storage — save your preferences locally
  • alarms — apply schedule start/end times
  • declarativeNetRequest (and host access) — block requests to sites you selected
  • Host permissions — only for the social/entertainment sites listed in the Extension so blocking and the interstitial page can work

Third parties

The Extension does not send data to third-party servers. Blocked sites are not contacted when a rule redirects you to the Extension’s blocked page.

Children

FocusGuard is a productivity tool and is not directed at children under 13. It does not knowingly collect children’s personal information.

Changes

If this policy changes, we will update the “Last updated” date on this page.

Contact

Questions about privacy: open an issue on the FocusGuard GitHub repository.
